# Publication Definition

## Working identity

**Name:** TBD  
**Working description:** A technical intelligence publication covering the systems, compute,
infrastructure, and security behind modern AI and software.

The final name must not be assumed until explicitly decided.

## Reader promise

Help technically sophisticated readers understand:

- what changed;
- what is actually new;
- what the numbers mean;
- what the source material does and does not establish;
- what technical or economic bottleneck moves;
- who or what is affected;
- what remains unknown.

## Editorial thesis

Most technology announcements are easy to summarize and difficult to interpret.

The publication competes on interpretation backed by evidence.

A reader should finish a strong story with at least one useful conclusion they were unlikely to
get from a press release, generic news rewrite, or one-paragraph chatbot answer.

## Content products

### Signal

Fast, evidence-backed technical update.

Typical length: 300–700 words.

Must answer:
1. What happened?
2. What materially changed?
3. Why does it matter?
4. What is not yet known?

### Breakdown

Focused technical analysis.

Typical length: 900–2,000 words.

Must go beyond the announcement.

### Deep Dive

High-effort reference-quality analysis.

Typical length: 2,000–5,000+ words when justified.

Length is not a target. Completeness is.

### Data / Benchmark

Structured or reproducible technical information such as:

- LLM API pricing
- GPU specifications
- inference economics
- benchmark comparisons
- cloud availability
- model capability tracking

### Explainer

Evergreen reference that solves a real technical comprehension problem.

Do not produce generic glossary filler.

## What we are not

- a press-release rewriting service
- a high-volume SEO farm
- an affiliate listicle site
- an AI-tool directory
- a rumor mill
- a political news outlet
- a consumer gadget blog
- an investment-advice service

## Long-term moat

The publication should gradually accumulate:

- clean source history;
- structured technical datasets;
- proprietary comparisons;
- reproducible calculations;
- benchmark context;
- historical timelines;
- durable editorial expertise.

The public site is the distribution surface. The newsroom repository is the operating knowledge
asset.
