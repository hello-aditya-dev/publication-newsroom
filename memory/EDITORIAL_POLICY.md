# Editorial Policy

## Core standard

Publish only when the story is accurate, relevant to the defined audience, and adds useful
understanding.

Being first is valuable only when we can still be correct.

## Independence

Editorial conclusions are not for sale.

Advertising or sponsorship must never determine factual conclusions, benchmark outcomes, review
judgments, or story selection presented as independent editorial work.

Sponsored material must be clearly labeled.

## AI use

AI may assist with:
- discovery;
- source organization;
- research extraction;
- comparison;
- calculation;
- draft generation;
- editing;
- consistency checks;
- metadata preparation.

AI does not remove editorial accountability.

The newsroom must not:
- invent human reporters;
- fabricate interviews;
- fabricate firsthand experience;
- claim independent testing that did not occur;
- claim human authorship where the publication's own disclosure policy would make that misleading;
- use AI merely to mass-produce low-value pages.

## Original reporting vs analysis

Label the nature of the work accurately.

If a story is based entirely on public primary sources, do not imply direct access, an interview,
or independent laboratory testing.

If analysis includes an estimate, show assumptions where material.

## Corrections

Material factual errors must be corrected promptly.

A correction should:
1. fix the body;
2. update the modification time;
3. preserve a concise correction note when the error materially affected reader understanding.

Do not silently rewrite major conclusions after publication.

## Anonymous / confidential sources

Not part of the Phase 0 operating model.

If later introduced, create a dedicated source-protection policy before using them.

## Rumors and leaks

Do not publish a rumor solely because it is circulating.

For leaked information:
- assess provenance;
- seek corroboration;
- clearly label status;
- avoid laundering speculation through confident prose.

## Embargoes

Respect agreed embargoes.

Do not enter embargo agreements without human-editor involvement.

## Conflicts of interest

Disclose material conflicts that could reasonably affect reader interpretation.

## Safety and responsible handling

Do not publish:
- active credentials;
- private personal data;
- secrets obtained accidentally;
- exploit instructions whose primary value is enabling harm rather than informing defenders.

Security coverage should provide enough technical detail to understand risk and mitigation while
avoiding unnecessary weaponization.

## Kill criteria

Reject or pause a story when:
- the underlying event is trivial;
- the angle is indistinguishable from dozens of rewrites;
- primary evidence cannot support the central claim;
- the headline outruns the evidence;
- the only available contribution is generic summary;
- the factual risk is disproportionate to reader value;
- the subject lies outside the publication's focus without a compelling reason.
