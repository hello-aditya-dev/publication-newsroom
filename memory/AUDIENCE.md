# Audience

## Primary readers

### 1. Software and infrastructure engineers

They care about:
- architecture;
- reliability;
- APIs;
- latency;
- throughput;
- cost;
- deployment constraints;
- operational tradeoffs.

### 2. AI / ML engineers and researchers

They care about:
- model/system architecture;
- training and inference;
- evaluations;
- memory and compute;
- serving;
- hardware;
- reproducibility.

### 3. Engineering leaders and CTOs

They care about:
- strategic technical consequences;
- build-vs-buy tradeoffs;
- cost structure;
- vendor risk;
- adoption timing;
- reliability and security.

### 4. Technical founders and product leaders

They care about:
- platform shifts;
- capability changes;
- new constraints;
- infrastructure economics;
- defensibility;
- ecosystem movement.

### 5. Technical investors and industry analysts

Secondary audience only. They care about:
- competitive implication;
- capital intensity;
- unit economics;
- supply constraints;
- technology roadmaps.

The publication does not provide individualized financial advice.

## Geographic strategy

Write for a global English-language technical audience.

Do not unnecessarily localize technical reporting to one country when the underlying story is
global.

## Reader sophistication

Assume the core reader understands software and modern technology.

Do not explain common engineering terms simply to inflate an article. Explain specialized concepts
when comprehension depends on them.

## Reader jobs-to-be-done

A strong article helps the reader do at least one of:

- understand an announcement faster;
- decide whether a technology matters;
- compare competing systems;
- estimate costs;
- identify deployment implications;
- understand benchmark limitations;
- identify a changed bottleneck;
- explain the development to a technical team;
- locate authoritative primary evidence.

## What destroys trust

- fabricated precision;
- unexplained certainty;
- generic hype;
- disguised advertising;
- incorrect technical terminology;
- repeated factual errors;
- citing weak summaries instead of primary evidence;
- hiding uncertainty;
- sensational headlines unsupported by the article.
